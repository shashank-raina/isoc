What opting in actually gets you

On E5, E7 or the Defender suite. Checked against Microsoft's published documentation in October 2026, which is still moving.

Included
  • Microsoft security data from Defender for Endpoint, Office 365, Identity, Cloud Apps and Cloud, with no workspace ingestion charge
  • Entra ID Protection logs, plus Azure activity and audit logs and Office 365 activity and audit logs through their connectors
  • 90 days of retention on that data, against 30 in Defender today
  • Custom detections as the rule engine, authored and run in the Defender portal
  • Cases, workbooks and UEBA, at no extra cost
  • Playbooks written for you by an agent. These no longer consume Security Compute Units
  • A built-in data lake for cheap long-term storage, with advanced hunting over what sits in it
Costs extra
  • Non-Microsoft data at $2.40 a gigabyte. That is the carrot: roughly half the Sentinel pay-as-you-go rate
  • Extending Defender retention to 180 days, at $1.20 a gigabyte
  • Microsoft Fabric capacity, if you want the advanced analytics over lake data. Separate product, separate bill
  • Retention past the included window, and anything you promote back into the analytics tier
  • An active Azure subscription. The workspace still lands in one, even though you create it from the Defender portal
Not published yet
  • Whether SigninLogs, non-interactive sign-ins, AuditLogs, MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs and Azure diagnostic logs are included. None of them are named anywhere
  • What Perception agents will cost
  • Whether the Sentinel MCP server reaches an ISOC workspace, which decides how you connect your own agents to the data
Work you inherit
  • Defender raw logs never arrive in a workspace, so detections over them are custom detections in the portal and not analytics rules. Content built the old way gets rewritten
  • Automation rules with incident or alert triggers are planned rather than shipped. Alerts without incidents, and alert suppression, are not supported
  • Cross-workspace rules stay where they are
  • Playbook code an agent wrote runs with real permissions, so it needs review, least privilege and change control like any other code
How this is calculated

Staying on Sentinel. Your Defender raw logs and the unconfirmed Microsoft tables are both paid today. The benefit allowance, seats multiplied by 5 MB a day, is applied against that Microsoft total first. Whatever is left, plus all your non-Microsoft data, is billed at the Sentinel rate.

No Sentinel today. There is no current bill to compare against, so the page prices ISOC on its own. You have no benefit allowance to give up either, which removes the thing that makes this decision hard for existing Sentinel customers.

ISOC, best case. Defender data is not ingested into a workspace at all, so it is not a line on the bill. The unconfirmed tables are assumed to be covered by the included Microsoft data. Only your non-Microsoft data is billed, at the ISOC rate.

ISOC, worst case. Same, except the unconfirmed tables fall under additional Microsoft and non-Microsoft security data and are billed at the ISOC rate alongside your third-party feeds.

Months are 30.44 days. Figures are ingestion only and exclude retention, the data lake, Fabric capacity and anything priced per user.

Why there are two ISOC figures

Microsoft names the supported Microsoft security data specifically: Defender for Endpoint, Office 365, Identity, Cloud Apps and Cloud, Microsoft Entra Identity Protection logs, Azure Activity and audit logs through a connector, and Office 365 Activity and audit logs through a connector.

Entra Identity Protection logs are the risk detection table. They are not SigninLogs, and they are not non-interactive sign-ins, which in most tenants are among the largest tables in the workspace. MicrosoftGraphActivityLogs and Azure diagnostic logs are not named anywhere either.

Those unnamed tables fall into the next section of the same documentation page, covering additional Microsoft and non-Microsoft security data through the 500+ connectors, where additional ingestion charges might apply.

So they are either free or they are $2.40 a gigabyte, and that is a question for your account team rather than for a calculator.

What this model deliberately leaves out
  • Retention beyond the included window, and the data lake tier
  • Microsoft Fabric capacity, which is a separate purchase
  • The engineering cost of moving detection content, which is usually larger than the ingestion difference in year one
  • Commitment tier discounts, unless you lower the Sentinel rate yourself
  • Anything announced after this page was written

A lower bill is not the same as a better outcome. If moving costs you a detection capability you rely on, the saving is not a saving.
